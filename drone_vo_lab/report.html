What is being compared?

Baseline detects Shi-Tomasi corners in every previous frame, tracks them with pyramidal Lucas-Kanade, backprojects metric depth, and estimates motion with RANSAC PnP and pose refinement.

Checked uses the same pipeline, with forward/backward flow rejection and minimum inlier-count and inlier-ratio checks.

Recovery uses the checked pipeline but retains its RGB-D reference after rejection. It attempts to recover motion across the gap. After the configured failure limit, it re-anchors at the held pose and marks the gap as unrecovered. Earlier held poses are never rewritten.

All accumulate camera motion in SE(3). The estimator receives only RGB, depth, and calibration. It has no access to ground-truth poses, scene geometry, or motion commands.

Recovery needs sufficient overlap with the retained reference. It is not global relocalization or loop closure. Accepted recovery estimates can still be wrong, and blackout gaps can remain lost.

Scope and limitations

This is a synthetic RGB-D visual-odometry benchmark with classical computer vision. It is not a monocular SLAM system, a learned policy, a flight controller, or a real-world drone deployment.

Depth is ideal simulator axial depth. Blur is a post-render horizontal filter, not a physical shutter model. Frame drops remove synchronized RGB-D pairs; the original timestamps are preserved. All scenes and trajectories are procedural.

Timing measures estimator work on the recorded machine, excluding image rendering, corruption, plotting, and video encoding. It is not an onboard-compute guarantee. Numerical results may vary across OpenCV versions and render backends.
